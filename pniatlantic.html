Cape Breton athletes competing in Special Olympics Canada Summer Games

Amongst them are four soccer players and one track-and-field athlete, as well as soccer coach Donna White and powerlifting coach Melissa Nearing.

Article content
Article content

“It’s really a historical team,” said White. “They’ve never had a female soccer team represent Nova Scotia before and competed in a women’s soccer division at nationals. That’s a big step.”

Article content
Article content

The soccer players from Cape Breton are Tara Black, Sabrina Sanson, Marie Claude McMahon, and Kellan Deruelle. Most of the soccer players have been playing for over a decade or more.

Article content
Article content

“I’m definitely excited,” said Black, a defender who has played soccer for over 15 years.

Article content

When asked about what has kept her involved in the support, she said, “Friends.”

Article content

Sanson, who plays either winger or defence, has been playing for around 19 years. She identified a few reasons why she’s played the sport for so long.

Article content
Article content

“I like the sport, I like staying healthy, and I like meeting new people,” said Sanson.

Article content

McMahon, a goalie with experience in several other positions, answered similarly to Black, saying it was a sense of fun that led her to play for over 20 years.

Article content
Article content

Deruelle said she was mostly excited, but also a little nervous.

Article content
Article content

Anneka Callary will represent the region in track and field. She said she started athletics around four years ago. Her favourite activities are the 100-metre and 200-metre run, and long jumping.

Article content
Article content

“Just hanging out with my friends is what I’m looking forward to,” she said.

Article content
Article content

SUPPORTING EACH OTHER

Article content

Some of these athletes attend Cape Breton Special Olympics events more than once a week, whether it be for their regular soccer practice or matches, or in other sports and activities.

Article content

“A number of the athletes who were here tonight for practice were playing tennis last night,” said White.

Article content

This has led to some close friendships, ones that have only expanded as the athletes get to know their Team Nova Scotia teammates from across the province through shared practices, video calls, and more.

Article content

“I think this is what sport is meant to be about. Supporting each other, really cheering each other on,” said White.

Article content

Being the first women’s soccer team from Nova Scotia competing in the Special Olympics makes this a great opportunity for these athletes to form connections with other women, empowering each other both on and off the field.

Article content

“Even just being there and having the opportunity to compete with other females, to build confidence in their skill and to cheer one another on and have fun, make memories,” said White. “Those are the big things for me, having a positive experience while they’re there.“

Her expectations of the team are those of any coach: to see her players express good sportsmanship, respect, and improve their soccer skills along the way through testing themselves against others.

Article content
Article content

REWARDING EXPERIENCE

Article content

White has been coaching soccer since she was 14, and has been doing so with Cape Breton Special Olympics for around 15 years.

Article content

“It’s a very rewarding volunteer experience,” she said. “It’s the best of sport. You see the athletes succeed, and it could be something as simple as catching a ball for the first time. Then you look over at the stands and you see the parent who’s crying because they just saw their child do a milestone for the first time.

Article content

“It’s seeing those impactful moments and knowing how hard they all work, and how dedicated they are to the sports they play.”

Article content

In the days leading up to their departure for Medicine Hat, White’s vigour for her team and the athletes she’s coached week after week has only grown stronger.

Article content

“One of the best decisions I’ve ever made is to coach Special Olympics,” said White. “It’s brought a lot of joy, and the athletes are great teachers on life and what’s important.”
