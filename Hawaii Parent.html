Island Events & Oahu Family Fun Calendar: August 2026

22nd Annual Korean Festival

Saturday, August 8, 10:00 AM – 8:00 PM

Celebrate Korean culture through a full day of live entertainment, cultural performances, authentic Korean food, and family-friendly activities. Festival highlights include a K-pop concert, hanbok fashion show, K-beauty experiences, kids’ activities, and appearances by Korean performers, musicians, and special guests. Explore the sights, sounds, and flavors of Korea while experiencing the culture and traditions that bring Hawaii’s diverse community together.

For more information, visit koreanfesthawaii.com.

HNL X Fandom Expo

Saturday, August 8 – Sunday, August 9

Explore a weekend celebrating pop culture with anime, cosplay, K-pop, gaming, collectibles, and artist vendors all under one roof. Browse unique merchandise, watch cosplay showcases, cheer on esports and Beyblade tournaments, discover local artists, and enjoy live entertainment throughout the event.

For more information, visit pacificexpos.com.

Duke’s OceanFest

Friday, August 14 – Monday, August 24

Duke’s OceanFest is a vibrant celebration of Hawaii’s ocean sports, culture, and aloha spirit. Held in honor of legendary waterman and Olympic champion Duke Kahanamoku, the festival brings together athletes, families, and ocean enthusiasts for a variety of exciting competitions and activities.

Visitors can enjoy surfing, swimming, paddling, beach volleyball, and other ocean-inspired events that showcase the skills and traditions deeply rooted in Hawaii’s connection to the sea. The festival also features live entertainment, cultural presentations, and opportunities to learn more about Duke’s lasting impact on Hawaii and the world of water sports.

With activities for all ages, Duke’s OceanFest offers families a chance to experience the energy of Waikiki while celebrating the values Duke embodied throughout his life: sportsmanship, community, and aloha. Whether cheering on competitors, enjoying the beach atmosphere, or participating in family-friendly activities, attendees will find plenty of ways to connect with Hawaii’s rich ocean heritage and the enduring legacy of one of its most beloved ambassadors.

For more information, visit dukekahanamokuoceanfest.com.

PHAMily Fun Day: End of Summer

Saturday, August 15, 10:00 AM – 4:00 PM

Wrap up summer with a family-friendly day of interactive activities at Pearl Harbor Aviation Museum. Explore historic aircraft and exhibits while enjoying special hands-on experiences designed for keiki and families, making it a fun way to celebrate the season before school is back in full swing.

For more information, visit pearlharboraviationmuseum.org.

Ewa Carnival

Saturday, August 15 – Sunday, August 16

It’s back! Experience a weekend of carnival excitement with thrilling rides, classic midway games, live entertainment, and a variety of local food vendors at the Wai Kai Events Lawn. Families can enjoy pony rides, a petting zoo, and attractions for all ages, making it a fun-filled outing with plenty of opportunities to play, explore, and win prizes.

For more information, visit tmrevents.net.

Hawaii Spartan Trifecta Weekend

Saturday, August 15 – Sunday, August 16

Challenge yourself on a rugged obstacle course at Dillingham Ranch, where steep climbs, coastal views, muddy trails, and natural terrain test endurance and strength. With race distances for beginners through elite athletes, plus a kids race, the weekend offers an unforgettable adventure for competitors of all ages.

For more information, visit spartan.com.

32nd Annual Made in Hawaii Festival

Thursday, August 20 – Sunday, August 23

The Annual Made in Hawaii Festival is one of the state’s most beloved celebrations of local creativity, culture, and entrepreneurship. Bringing together hundreds of vendors from across the islands, the festival showcases the incredible talent and innovation that make Hawaii unique.

Families can browse a wide variety of locally made products, including handcrafted jewelry, artwork, home goods, fashion, specialty foods, gifts, and island-inspired creations. It’s a wonderful opportunity to support local businesses while discovering one-of-a-kind items that cannot be found anywhere else.

The festival offers a vibrant atmosphere filled with live entertainment, cultural demonstrations, and opportunities to connect with Hawaii’s diverse community of makers and artisans. Food lovers can sample local flavors, while keiki and adults alike can enjoy the festive energy that makes this event a favorite annual tradition.

Whether searching for unique gifts, supporting local entrepreneurs, or simply enjoying a day immersed in island culture, the Made in Hawaii Festival provides an unforgettable experience that highlights the creativity, craftsmanship, and aloha spirit found throughout Hawaii.

For more information, visit madeinhawaiifestival.com.

End of Summer Spectacular

Saturday, August 22, 10:30 AM – 9:00 PM

Celebrate the end of summer at Wet’n’Wild Hawaii with a full day of waterpark fun, live entertainment, a glow foam dance party, family activities, and a dive-in movie. Enjoy local food vendors, prize giveaways, and an evening fireworks spectacular, while supporting local families through a community flip-flop donation drive.

For more information, visit wetnwildhawaii.com.

Waimea Valley’s Moonwalk

Sunday, August 23, 8:00 PM – 10:00 PM

Experience Waimea Valley after dark on a guided moonlit walk through the lush valley. Learn about Hawaiian culture through ʻike sharing, explore the traditional kauhale on a cultural tour, and end the evening at the illuminated waterfall while taking in the sights, sounds, and scents of the valley at night.

For more information, visit waimeavalley.net.

Honolulu Rainbow Ekiden

Saturday, August 29, 8:00 AM

Experience a unique team relay race inspired by Japan’s traditional ekiden, where runners pass a ribbon lei instead of a baton along a scenic course around Diamond Head and Kapiʻolani Park. The event also features a 5K fun run and walk, a keiki challenge, and a festive atmosphere that welcomes participants and spectators of all ages.

For more information, visit rainbowekiden.com.

12th Annual Waimea Valley Summer Concert

Saturday, August 29, 4:00 PM – 7:00 PM

The 12th Annual Waimea Valley Summer Concert Series invites families to enjoy an unforgettable evening of music, culture, and community in one of Oahu’s most beautiful outdoor settings. This year’s event features an outstanding lineup of Hawaii musicians, including multiple Na Hoku Hanohano Award winner Mark Yamanaka, beloved entertainer Jon Yamasato, acclaimed ukulele virtuoso Herb Ohta, Jr., and island music favorite Norm Thompson.

Adding to the celebration, featured performances by Halau I Kalima Kuhi Lani under the direction of Kumu Kalimakuhilani Akemi Kalamanamana Suganuma Southard and Christopher “TC” Southard will showcase the beauty and tradition of Hawaiian hula.

Guests can relax on the lawn, enjoy a picnic with family and friends, and experience an evening filled with exceptional music and cultural expression. Surrounded by the lush beauty and rich history of Waimea Valley, the concert offers a unique opportunity to connect with Hawaii’s artistic traditions while creating lasting summer memories.

Blending award-winning entertainment, Hawaiian culture, and a welcoming family atmosphere, the Waimea Valley Summer Concert Series continues to be a cherished North Shore tradition.

For more information, visit waimeavalley.net.

50th Annual Honolulu Intertribal Pow Wow

Saturday, August 29 – Sunday, August 30, 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM

The 50th Annual Honolulu Intertribal Pow Wow returns to Magic Island at Ala Moana Beach Park for a weekend of culture, tradition, and community. The event welcomes families to enjoy two days of cultural sharing and entertainment, with activities running from 9 AM to 5 PM on Saturday and 9 AM to 4 PM on Sunday.

This year’s celebration features Host Drum Scout Society, with Brad Bearsheart (Hunkpapa Lakota/Santee Dakota) serving as Emcee and Eric Lonechief (Skidi Pawnee/Chickasaw) as Arena Director. Attendees will also have the opportunity to watch Head Man Dancer Troy Delaney (Kainai Nation) and Head Lady Dancer Gabi Kenny (Sicangu Lakota), whose leadership and participation help bring the traditions of the Pow Wow to life.

Visitors can experience the beauty and significance of traditional dancing, drumming, music, and storytelling while learning about the diverse customs and histories of Native American, Alaska Native, First Nations, and Pacific Island communities. Colorful regalia, powerful drum groups, cultural demonstrations, arts and crafts, and traditional foods create an engaging experience for all ages.

For more information, visit htchawaii.org.

Ka Maiʻa Hoʻolauleʻa Banana Festival

Sunday, August 30, 10:00 AM – 2:30 PM

Celebrate Hawaiʻi’s beloved banana varieties with a unique community festival featuring a banana fiber fashion show, educational presentations, and opportunities to learn about the cultural and agricultural importance of maiʻa. Enjoy a fun, family-friendly event highlighting local knowledge, sustainability, and the many uses of bananas.

For more information, visit slowfoodoahu.com.
