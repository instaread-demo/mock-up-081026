By Laura SebbenReporter

When Randy Mathura’s mother happened to check the security cameras at their family-owned jewelry store overnight Wednesday, she was shocked to see there had been a break-in.

“Alarms were going off after they broke the windows, came through the store and smashed the glass cases,” Mathura recounted to The Spectator.

He rushed to the store and had to call police himself after the security and alarm companies used for the business at 171 Mohawk Rd. E., a small plaza near Upper Wellington Street, failed to do so.

Hamilton police confirmed its BEAR (break, enter, auto theft and robbery) unit is investigating a commercial break and enter at Gold Forever Jewellery.

Security footage shared by Mathura and viewed by The Spectator shows two people attempting to smash the glass on the front door of the business just after 3 a.m., according to the time stamp.

One person appears to be kicking the door, while the other seems to be hitting it with an object. Alarms can be heard sounding.

They eventually give up and what looks like a white sedan reverses directly into the storefront, breaking the windows and dislodging the metal grates behind them.

After the sedan pulls away, at least five people enter the store. On camera, they can be seen smashing glass display cases and removing merchandise from inside them before leaving.

All the events unfolded in less than two minutes.

“They got away with some inventory, but we’re just happy that no one was actually here and everyone’s safe,” Mathura said.

He estimates the suspects stole between $10,000 and $20,000 worth of products.

Now, the family is working to clean up the store, replace the storefront windows and get new glass cases for the jewelry.

Despite the ongoing work, Gold Forever Jewellery remains open.

“We put some plywood up and the lights are still working. We have no air conditioning at the moment so it’s a bit hot in the store, but we went and bought fans,” Mathura explained, saying they’re one of the only shops in Hamilton that offers same-day jewelry repairs and wanted to remain available for their customers.

Moving forward, Mathura said they plan to switch security and alarm companies. The family also plans to upgrade their cameras and install concrete bollards in front of the store to try to prevent someone from being able to drive into the windows again.

The investigation is ongoing, police said, and no arrests have been made.

Wednesday’s break-in follows a pattern of similar, organized jewelry store robberies across Hamilton and surrounding areas in recent years, including suspects acting quickly to smash display cases and grab what they can and in some cases the use of vehicles to gain entry.

Both standalone businesses and mall stores have been targeted.

The fallout has led to higher insurance costs and the need for all businesses to step up security measures, according to experts interviewed by The Spectator.

Ancaster Jewellers on Wilson Street West added electronic doors, steel-reinforced walls, two safes, a smokescreen alarm, bollards and other security features after the business was a victim of this style of attack twice in one year in December 2024 and November 2025.
