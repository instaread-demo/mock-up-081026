FIA confirms Bahrain Grand Prix at Sepang will be a daytime race after start-time confusion

By Balazs Szabo on 07 Aug 2026, 03:00
Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by
0:00 / 2:09
Instaread

The FIA has officially confirmed that this year's Bahrain Grand Prix at Malaysia's Sepang International Circuit will be held in daylight, putting an end to speculation that Formula 1's unique relocated event could become a night race.

Formula One announced during the Hungarian Grand Prix weekend that the Bahrain Grand Prix would be staged at Sepang after the original event in Sakhir was cancelled earlier this season due to the conflict in the Middle East.

The race will become the first Formula 1 World Championship Grand Prix to retain the name of one country while being held in another.

F1 app sparked confusion

Confusion emerged shortly after the revised calendar was published when the official Formula 1 app listed the race as beginning at 8:00pm local time—identical to Bahrain's traditional night-race schedule.

That led many fans to believe Sepang would host its first-ever Formula 1 night race, despite the Malaysian circuit never previously staging a Grand Prix under floodlights.

However, the FIA has now released an updated official timetable confirming that the event will instead follow a traditional daytime schedule.

Qualifying will begin at 4:00pm local time on Saturday, while Sunday's 56-lap Bahrain Grand Prix will start at 3:00pm local time, meaning the race will be run entirely in daylight.

Sepang returns after nine years

The event marks Formula 1's return to the Sepang International Circuit for the first time since 2017.

Although the venue is once again part of the calendar, it will officially be known as the Bahrain Grand Prix, following an agreement between Formula One Management, the FIA and the governments of Bahrain and Malaysia to preserve Bahrain's place on the 2026 calendar after the original race was cancelled.

The daytime start is likely to be welcomed by many drivers, with Sepang renowned for its intense heat, high humidity and unpredictable tropical weather.

Afternoon thunderstorms have often played a major role in previous Malaysian Grands Prix, adding another strategic element to one of Formula 1's most demanding circuits.
